Turn an old phone into a permanent motorcycle navigation screen.

A slim sealed phone case that slides onto a handlebar dock, locks with a key and charges through spring contacts. No cable to plug in. Designed for the Royal Enfield Meteor 350, and adjustable for most bikes with a round handlebar.

Download everything (.zip) View on GitHub

Free to download, print and modify.

Prototype status: this is a dimensioned concept model, version 0.5. It has not yet been tested on the road, for water, heat or vibration. Check the fit on your own bike and phone before relying on it, and use a safety tether at first.

How it works

The video is rendered straight from the CAD: assembly, docking, the contacts, the wiring, the key lock and the over-heat cut-off.

Slim sealed case

The phone drops in from behind. A polycarbonate window in front, an O-ring back cover and 4 hidden screws keep the rain out.

Slide-on dock

Dovetail rails guide the case down until flat pads on its nose press onto 10 spring pins. A printed snap holds it there.

Key lock

A quarter turn pushes a flat 14 × 9 mm steel bolt into the case. The keyhole is on the side, behind the top corner, clear of the tank.

Safe charging

The pins are dead until a case is docked. A 45 °C thermostat behind the phone cuts charging if it gets too hot, and it resumes by itself once cool.

Status LEDs

Yellow: bike power. Green: charging. Red: docked but not charging (too hot, or a bad contact).

Modular

Clamps, base plate, dock and case are separate. Change the tilt by rotating the clamps, and swap the base plate for a "smart base" later.

The STLs are made for the default (placeholder) phone, 75 × 160 × 9 mm, and a 22.2 mm handlebar. For your own phone and bike, change the measurements in the source and export your own parts: see Adjust it to your hardware. The parts that depend on the phone are case_shell, case_back and window_template, and the clamps and shims depend on the bar.

Adjust it to your hardware

Every dimension lives in one file, phone_dock_v0.5.scad. Values marked MEASURE must be measured on your phone or bike, and values marked SELECT depend on the part you buy. The defaults are placeholders, so don't print the case before you set them.

  1. Install OpenSCAD (2021.01 or newer). It is free.
  2. Open phone_dock_v0.5.scad, change the values below near the top of the file, and press F5 to preview the whole assembly. The file stops with a clear message if a value makes parts collide.
  3. Export each part. Set PART to the part's name, press F6, then File → Export → STL. Or from a terminal: 
    openscad -D 'PART="case_shell"' -o case_shell.stl phone_dock_v0.5.scad
openscad -D 'PART="case_back"'  -D 'phone_w=71.5' -D 'phone_l=146.7' -o case_back.stl phone_dock_v0.5.scad
Your phoneSets the case
phone_w, phone_l, phone_tWidth (short side), length and thickness in mm. Measure with calipers, without a cover. MEASURE
phone_corner_rThe corner radius seen from the front. MEASURE
screen_maskHow far the front lip overlaps the phone's edge. Set it to the phone's own black border so the lip doesn't cover the screen.
camera_x, camera_y, camera_w, camera_h, camera_protrusionPosition and size of the camera bump, measured in portrait from the phone's centre. Set camera_relief_enable = false for a flat back.
phone_port"usbc" or "microusb". Micro-USB makes the plug bay 9 mm instead of 8.
usb_sideIn landscape: 1 if the charging port is on the right, -1 if on the left.
usb_port_yMove it if the port isn't centred on the phone's end (most are: 0).
usb_bay_hRoom for the 90° plug. The plug head must be no more than 7 mm (USB-C) or 8 mm (micro-USB) from the phone's edge; raise this if yours is bigger.
Your handlebar and bikeSets the clamps and height
bar_dHandlebar diameter at the clamps: 22.2 (7/8"), 25.4 (1") or 28.6 mm (1-1/8"). Export bar_shim to match, or wrap strips of old inner tube. MEASURE
clamp_spacingDistance between the two clamps: 110–154 mm with the standard base plate. It needs straight, round bar on both sides of the centre. MEASURE
riser_h, riser_dYour bike's own handlebar clamp: how high its highest bolt head sits above the top of the bar, and how far its far edge is from the bar's centre. The file then sets the lowest safe mounting height by itself. MEASURE
tiltScreen angle, for the preview only. On the bike you set it by rotating both clamps to the same angle.
arm_backMoves the mount fore or aft.
spacer_hRaises the mount with pad_spacer blocks if the instrument cluster needs more room.
slot_out, hide_plateFor clamps wider than 154 mm apart: raise slot_out and set hide_plate = false. The plate will then show past the case.

Check full steering lock both ways: nothing may touch the tank, the mirrors or the cables.

The key lockMatch the lock you buy
lock_bore_d, lock_bore_flatLock thread diameter (16 mm by default) and its width across the flats. SELECT
lock_body_lenFrom the inside face of the panel to the cam seat. SELECT
lock_nut_clear_dThe lock nut across its corners, plus clearance. SELECT
crank_tThickness of the lock's own steel cam, which becomes the crank. Drill it using the printed crank_template. SELECT
bolt_w, bolt_tThe steel bolt: 14 × 9 mm parallel-key steel by default. Change these if you use a different size. SELECT
steel_keeperSet it to true for a laser-cut 6 mm steel strike (strike_keeper_2d.dxf) instead of the printed one with a steel block. It is stronger, but needs a fabricator.

Use a 16 mm tubular cam lock that turns a quarter turn, with a key that comes out only when locked.

Contacts and electronicsMatch your pins, cable and sensor
pin_block_depth, pin_freePogo pin length (P75 pins are 15.85 mm) and how far the tips stick out. Change them for other pins. SELECT
cable_dHole for the bike's USB lead (4.2 mm suits most leads, which are 3.5–4 mm thick).
therm_l, therm_w, therm_tSize of your thermostat (KSD9700, 45 °C, normally closed). Sizes vary by seller, so measure yours. SELECT
thermal_cutoffSet it to false to leave out the over-heat cut-off. Then link DET to GND on the pad strip.
led_dLight-pipe hole for the status LEDs (3 mm LEDs need 3.2 mm).

The dock board can be hand-wired on perfboard or made as a small ordered PCB. It is the same circuit, the same parts and the same pocket either way.

Fasteners and fitMatch your inserts and printer
corner_insert_d, shoe_insert_dHoles for M3 heat-set inserts (4.2 mm). Check your inserts' recommended hole size. SELECT
m5_insert_dHoles for the M5 inserts (clamps, plate, AMPS pattern).
flex_tStiffness of the printed snap that holds the case. If the case creeps up the rails with the lock open, add 0.2 mm.
window_tWindow thickness: use 2 mm polycarbonate, not acrylic (it cracks).
drain_dSize of the drain holes at the bottom of the rail channels.

Electronics and wiring

The bike's USB lead is wired straight into the dock. A fuse and a MOSFET switch put 5 V on the pins only while a case is docked and cool. USB data passes through, with ESD protection. There are 10 pogo pins in 2 rows: power, data, detect, doubled power contacts and 2 spares for later.

Build it

  1. Mock up the case in cardboard (178 × 85 mm) on your bike. Measure the bar and choose the clamp spacing.
  2. Measure your phone, set the values above and export your parts.
  3. Print in PETG or ASA. You don't need a printer of your own: a local print service works. Print the clamps at 100 % infill, and the dock back face down.
  4. Buy the hardware: a 16 mm cam lock, 14 × 9 mm key steel, 10 P75 pogo pins, a 45 °C thermostat, a few cheap electronic parts, inserts and screws.
  5. Solder the dock board (perfboard or PCB), the pin strip and the pad strip, then pot them in clear epoxy.
  6. Test on the bench: LEDs, docking, the lock and the heat cut-off. Then spray-test it, ride without the phone, and finally ride with it.
